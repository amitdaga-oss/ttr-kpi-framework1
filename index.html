SECTION 1

The Business Context

As we scale our support organization, engineers are increasingly expected to handle multiple case types — Support, Success Guide, and Onboarding Specialist cases — each with different complexity levels and resolution time expectations.

This creates a critical challenge: how do we fairly measure and rank engineer performance when their case mix varies month to month?

SECTION 2 · STEP 1 OF 3

The Problem with Raw TTR

A simple average TTR (Time to Resolve) is misleading when engineers handle cases with different KPI targets. Comparing raw numbers across case types is like comparing apples to oranges.

Defined KPI Targets by Case Type

Case TypeTTR Target
Support Engineer7 Days
Success Guide17 Days
Onboarding Specialist28 Days

Example — Why Raw TTR Fails

  • Engineer A closes 10 Support cases averaging 6 days each → raw TTR = 6 days Looks great
  • Engineer B closes 10 Onboarding cases averaging 25 days each → raw TTR = 25 days Looks bad
At face value, Engineer A looks far superior. But Engineer B is actually beating their target while Engineer A is also meeting theirs. Both are performing well — raw TTR hides this completely.
SECTION 3 · STEP 2 OF 3

The Solution — Normalized TTR Performance Score

Instead of comparing raw TTR, we convert each case into a TTR Performance Score that measures how well the engineer performed against the target for that specific case type.

Formula

TTR Performance Score (per case) = Target TTR ÷ Actual TTR
ScoreMeaning
> 1.0✓ Beat the target
= 1.0→ Exactly met the target
< 1.0✗ Missed the target

Example Calculations

Case TypeTarget TTRActual TTRScore
Support7 days5 days7 ÷ 5 = 1.40 ✓
Support7 days9 days7 ÷ 9 = 0.78 ✗
Success Guide17 days12 days17 ÷ 12 = 1.42 ✓
Onboarding28 days30 days28 ÷ 30 = 0.93 ✗
SECTION 4 · STEP 3 OF 3

Monthly Performance Score

For an engineer who handles X Support, Y Guide, and Z Onboarding cases in a month:

Monthly Score = Sum of all TTR Performance Scores ÷ Total Cases (X + Y + Z)

Expanded

Monthly Score = [ Σ(7 ÷ Actual_TTR) for each Support case + Σ(17 ÷ Actual_TTR) for each Guide case + Σ(28 ÷ Actual_TTR) for each Onboarding case ] ÷ (X + Y + Z)

This naturally handles case mix imbalance — an engineer with 2 Support cases and 15 Onboarding cases won't be penalized or inflated just because of their case distribution.

SECTION 5

Volume Weighting — Why Logarithmic?

The Problem Without Volume Weighting

Without accounting for volume, a low-volume engineer who closes just 2 easy cases perfectly could rank #1 over a high-volume engineer who closed 40 cases at 95% efficiency. That's unfair and misleading for leadership.

Why Not Linear Weighting?

A linear volume multiplier (Score × Total Cases) would swing too far in the other direction — an engineer with 50 cases would dominate the rankings even with mediocre performance, simply due to volume.

The Logarithmic Advantage

The logarithm grows fast initially (rewarding meaningful volume) but flattens out at higher volumes (preventing volume from dominating the score unfairly).

Total Caseslog(n+1)Effect
1 case0.69Low — small sample
5 cases1.79Moderate — meaningful
15 cases2.77Good — solid sample
30 cases3.43Strong — high volume
50 cases3.93Very strong — but only ~14% more than 30 cases

This means going from 1 to 15 cases dramatically boosts your adjusted score, but going from 30 to 50 cases gives only a marginal bump — keeping the focus on quality of performance, not just quantity.

The Formula

Adjusted Monthly Score = Monthly Performance Score × log(X + Y + Z + 1)

When to Use Log vs. Skip It — Decision Guide

ScenarioRecommendation
All engineers handle roughly equal case volumes each month✓ Skip log — Monthly Performance Score alone is sufficient. Same log multiplier cancels out.
Case volumes vary significantly across engineers (real-world norm)✓ Use log — Prevents low-volume outliers from topping the chart on a few lucky cases.
A new team just starting out with low case counts⚠ Use log cautiously — Low volumes amplify randomness. Consider a minimum case threshold (e.g., 5+ cases) before ranking.
You want the simplest model for quick reporting✓ Skip log — Monthly Performance Score is intuitive and still fair if volumes are balanced.
Rule of thumb: If the gap between your highest-volume and lowest-volume engineer in a month is less than 2× (e.g., 8 vs. 15 cases), log makes negligible difference. If the gap is 3× or more (e.g., 3 vs. 20 cases), log materially protects ranking fairness.

Proof — When Log Actually Changes Rankings

Here's a concrete example with unequal volumes to show log in action:

EngineerTotal CasesMonthly Perf. Scorelog(Cases+1)Adjusted ScoreRank (with log)Rank (without log)
Eng-A31.45log(4) = 1.3862.010🥈 2nd🥇 1st

Without log: Eng-A ranks #1 because 1.45 > 1.28 — but Eng-A only closed 3 cases.
With log: Eng-B ranks #1 because sustained performance across 25 cases is genuinely more valuable than 3 lucky resolutions.

This is exactly the scenario log is designed to protect against. Eng-A may have received 3 easy cases by chance. Eng-B consistently outperformed targets at scale — and the logarithmic adjustment correctly promotes them.

The logarithmic approach balances two competing goals: rewarding engineers who carry high volume AND ensuring that raw volume cannot mask poor performance quality.

SECTION 6

Blended Target TTR — For Stakeholder Reporting

If stakeholders need to see a single TTR number (not a score), we can back-calculate a personalized blended target for each engineer based on their actual case mix:

Blended Target TTR = (X × 7 + Y × 17 + Z × 28) ÷ (X + Y + Z)

Then compare the engineer's actual blended TTR against this personalized target. The gap tells the story:

  • ✓ Negative gap (actual < target) → Engineer is ahead of target
  • ✗ Positive gap (actual > target) → Engineer is behind target
SECTION 7

Ranking the Team of 10

At month end, every engineer has a single Adjusted Monthly Score. Higher = better.

Step-by-Step Ranking Process

  1. For each engineer, calculate the TTR Performance Score for every case they closed that month.
  2. Sum all scores and divide by total cases → Monthly Performance Score.
  3. Multiply by log(total cases + 1) → Adjusted Monthly Score.
  4. Rank all engineers from highest to lowest Adjusted Score.
  5. Flag any engineer with a Monthly Performance Score below 1.0 (missing targets on average).
SECTION 8

Key Takeaways for Leadership

This framework is fair, transparent, and scalable. It rewards engineers who consistently beat their case-type targets, accounts for volume without being dominated by it, and produces a single comparable score regardless of case mix.

✓ Fair
No engineer is penalized for receiving harder, longer case types.
✓ Transparent
Every score traces back to individual case performance.
✓ Scalable
Works whether the team has 10 or 100 engineers.
✓ Flexible
New case types can be added by simply defining a new TTR target.
✓ Actionable
Engineers with scores below 1.0 can be coached on specific case types.

SECTION 10

Summary for Leadership

The Framework in 3 Steps

  1. Normalize each case using Target ÷ Actual TTR
  2. Average all scores for the month
  3. Multiply by log(cases+1) for volume weight

What Leaders Get

  • One comparable score per engineer
  • Rankings immune to case mix bias
  • Volume recognized but not dominant
  • Transparent, auditable calculation

Future-Proofing

  • Add new case types easily
  • Adjust targets without rebuilding the model
  • Works at any team size
  • Can feed into dashboards or Salesforce reports
